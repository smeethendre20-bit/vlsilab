THEORY:

The inverter is universally accepted as the most basic logic gate doing a Boolean operation on a single input variable. Fig.2.1 depicts thea general structure of a CMOS inverter. As shown, the simple structure consists of a combination of a PMOS transistor at the top and a NMOS transistor at the bottom.

Figure 2. 1: CMOS Inverter

Figure 2.2: Characteristics of CMOS inverter

CMOS is also referred to as complementary-symmetry metal–oxide–semiconductor. The words "complementary-symmetry" refer to the fact that the typical digital design style with CMOS uses complementary and symmetrical pairs of p-type and n-type metal oxide semiconductor field effect transistors (MOSFETs) for logic functions. Two important characteristics of CMOS devices are high noise immunity and low static power consumption. Significant power is only drawn while the transistors in the CMOS device are switching between on and off states. Consequently, CMOS devices do not produce as much waste heat as other forms of logic, for example transistor transistor logic (TTL) or NMOS logic, which uses all n-channel devices without p-channel devices.
